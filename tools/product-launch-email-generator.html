What a product launch email should include

A launch email should quickly explain the product, the reason it exists, the customer benefit, and the next step. The best launch copy is specific enough to create interest but concise enough for mobile readers.

  • Open with the new product or collection name.
  • Explain the main benefit in plain language.
  • Highlight 2-4 specific features or use cases.
  • Add a clear call to action.
  • Make launch timing, availability, or offer details accurate.

Email angles to test

Try a founder note, problem-solution announcement, early access message, customer-requested feature angle, seasonal drop, or limited restock. Each angle should still lead back to the product value.

Simple launch e-mail structure

Use a short subject line, a preview text that names the benefit, one opening paragraph, 2-4 feature callouts, and a clear CTA. For ecommerce campaigns, link to the product page early so mobile readers do not need to hunt for the offer.

Launch email sequence

A simple product launch sequence can include a teaser, an announcement, a benefit-focused follow-up, and a last-call reminder. Keep the sequence short for smaller stores and make each message useful on its own.

  • Teaser: hint at the problem, audience, or use case.
  • Launch: announce the product with the core benefit and link.
  • Proof: show materials, features, reviews, FAQs, or use cases.
  • Reminder: mention timing, inventory, or offer details if they are real.

Launch email template

Subject: Meet [product name]. Preview: Built for [audience] who want [benefit]. Body: Introduce the product, explain the buyer problem, list 2-3 reasons it helps, and close with one direct call to action.

Before sending

Review subject line, preview text, product links, discount rules, inventory status, and image alignment. A good launch email feels coordinated with the product page and the rest of the campaign.
